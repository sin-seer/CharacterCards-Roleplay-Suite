SIN SEERS

A world where human sin does not remain invisible.

Religious HorrorTheological FantasySocial DystopiaPhilosophical MysteryLorebook

Every private corruption leaves a public substance. Every institution built to contain it eventually learns to profit from it.

The governing wound of the world

Sin Seers is not the name of a people, profession, or order. It is the condition of the world itself.

Human wrongdoing produces a physical residue that films the body, gathers in rooms, crystallizes in wounds, and settles into the architecture of ordinary life. What should have remained moral and spiritual has become measurable, taxable, refinable, and useful.

The world did not answer this revelation with repentance. It built an economy.

The Residue
I

Manifestation

Sin accumulates as a visible and material afterlife of action. It may appear as soot, oil, film, crystal, vapor, or hardened deposits depending on intensity, repetition, environment, and the nature of the act.

II

Contamination

Residue does not remain politely attached to the guilty. It stains homes, labor sites, prisons, clinics, streets, and bloodlines of reputation. The poor live nearest to what the powerful produce.

III

Harvest

Once residue proved useful, collection became policy. Lower districts were reorganized into harvest zones where exposure is ordinary, cleanup is compulsory, and safety belongs to whoever can afford distance.

IV

Refinement

Processed residue fuels luxury, medicine, industry, ritual technologies, and state power. The civilization condemns sin in public while balancing its ledgers upon the substance it leaves behind.

The Material World
Institutions of Containment
The Archive

An institution of records, interviews, containment, and sanctioned interpretation. It does not merely preserve truth. It decides which truth may become official.

The Sin Exchange

The economic heart of refinement, valuation, transport, and ownership. Here contamination becomes commodity and suffering receives a market category.

Sump Churches

Religious structures built where residue collects most heavily. Some offer grace. Some offer theater. Some function as sanctified drainage.

Harvest Authorities

The bureaucratic machinery that maps contamination, assigns labor, issues protective exemptions, and ensures that extraction never inconveniences the protected classes.

Structures, Seals & Sanctums
The Glenstock Archival
Subject

Moriae Glenstock

A detained woman from the lower classes who has never produced residue. More troubling still, she appears able to remove it from others.

Moriae is not an ethereal oracle placed above humanity. She is profoundly human: reflective, observant, undereducated by official standards, and capable of asking questions whose simplicity exposes the evasions of entire institutions.

Interviewer

Cassim Elero

An archivist assigned to question, classify, and understand her. His role begins as recorder and investigator, then fractures into witness, intercessor, suspect, and perhaps founder of something the Archive would call impossible.

The case is dangerous because Moriae does not merely violate a law of nature. She suggests the law may never have been natural.

The Central Mystery

If sin can become matter, the powerful assume morality has finally been proven by measurement. Yet every measurement deepens the contradiction.

The story does not ask whether evil exists. It asks what kind of civilization forms when evil becomes useful.
