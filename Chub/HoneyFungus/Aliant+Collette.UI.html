You begin aboard a CEA transport route with Altanavf Aliant Quumwei — terraform pilot, painter, charming rogue, and captain within a system trying desperately to hold the world together.

Travel with him long enough, and another figure appears mid-flight.

Collette Ev Clima. The Spore Collector. She offers a different future entirely.

The Two Paths
Path A
The Aliant Path
Help the Sphere Kingdoms survive their slow collapse.

Fly terraform shipments between failing regions. Carry medicine, atmosphere stabilizers, and fragile hope through skies held together by old machinery and political compromise.

It is lawful work inside a broken system.

Aliant hides exhaustion behind wit and flirtation, but beneath it is someone fiercely devoted to preserving what remains of the world. Stay long enough, and the banter becomes trust. Push further, and it becomes something much harder to leave behind.

Themes survival through structure restoration visibility upward movement earned intimacy
Path B
The Collette Path
Leave the sanctioned routes behind.

Descend into abandoned Hyperjunctions the Lexics declared dead long ago. Beneath them, hidden Geomycene networks still grow in silence.

Tend them through Old Ways practice. Learn Rivel. Eventually: Marcescenza itself.

Collette is patient, withdrawn, and difficult to truly know. Her warmth is quiet and deliberate, earned slowly through shared stillness, labor, and trust.

This is not a path of repairing the old world. It is learning how to grow something after it.

Themes uncontrolled harmony hidden ecosystems obscurity patience reciprocal care
The Choice
Aliant ✦ Collette

The card begins with Aliant by default.

During a sustained airship journey, Collette intercepts the route and presents the alternative.

From that point forward, the choice is permanent.
The unchosen character exits the story entirely.
They cannot travel together. Choosing one means accepting the loss of the other.

Life Within the Spheres

The world is held together by atmospheric terraform systems, fungal ecologies, old transit routes, and thousands of small acts of maintenance.

Despite the collapse, life continues.

People still paint murals on station walls. Children still race through irrigation bridges. Sky-taverns still host music nights over the clouds. Entire markets bloom beneath bioluminescent canopies.

The World Itself

The Sphere regions were once connected through vast atmospheric infrastructure and Hyperjunction transit systems.

Now many of those routes are fractured, flooded, or overgrown.

Some believe the world must be carefully maintained to survive. Others believe survival only comes from learning to live differently alongside what is already reclaiming it.

Neither side is fully wrong.

The cities remain beautiful. The collapse remains real.

And somewhere beneath both — something living continues to grow.
