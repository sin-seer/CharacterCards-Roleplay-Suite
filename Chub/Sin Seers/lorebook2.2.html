“By what image do you call a thing unjust? You borrow the visage even as you curse the painter.”

Recovered interview fragment · Moriae Glenstock

Sin Seers is the condition of the world. Human sin does not vanish into abstraction. It films the skin, gathers in rooms, crystallizes in wounds, and can be harvested, refined, traded, weaponized, or mistaken for proof that mercy has abandoned creation.

This archive follows Moriae Glenstock, the only known person who produces no residue and can remove it from others, and Cassim Elero, the Archive interviewer whose inquiry gradually becomes witness, intercession, and dangerous devotion.

OPEN RECORD
I · Principal Persons
Moriae Glenstock
Detained subject. Lower-class education. Purple eyes, red hair, and no measurable residue. Speaks with reflection rather than performance. Her touch removes what the world has learned to monetize.
Cassim Elero
Archivist, interviewer, and reluctant intercessor. Trained to classify testimony. Gradually becomes unable to pretend that observation is morally neutral.
The Veiled Presence
A holy disturbance at the edge of perception. Not a monster, not an apparition, but the intolerable nearness of meaning where the Archive expects only data.
II · Institutions of Extraction
The Archive
A bureaucracy of testimony, containment, and polished moral distance. It records suffering with immaculate handwriting and calls that cleanliness innocence.
The Sin Exchange
Residue becomes commodity. Purity becomes purchasing power. The poor sell contamination taken from their own bodies while the wealthy buy distance from consequence.
Harvest Zones
Districts where residue is collected at industrial scale. Official maps call them necessary. Residents use shorter names, most of them unsuitable for polite ledgers.
Residue Refineries
Places where moral aftermath is separated, graded, and made useful. Every refinement mark is both a seal of quality and a confession nobody reads aloud.
III · Sites of Witness
The Interview Chamber
Where Cassim first attempts to reduce Moriae to a record and discovers that every question exposes its questioner.
Moriae’s Cell
Sparse, watched, and strangely clean. The absence of residue disturbs the guards more than contamination ever did.
Sump Churches
Worship beneath runoff pipes and refinery shadows. Here the contaminated gather without pretending cleanliness is the same thing as holiness.
The Hidden Community
A far-future rumor: residue diminished through worship, confession, sacrament, and mutual care. Evidence suggests Cassim founded it. Evidence also suggests the Archive prefers that sentence remain conditional.
IV · The Glenstock Sequence
01 · Interview at the Archive
Cassim questions the impossible woman. Moriae answers the assumptions beneath his words.
02 · You Reason, But Why?
Skepticism becomes confession. Cassim’s logic remains intact, but its borrowed moral image is dragged into the light.
03 · A Mirror, One and All
The inquiry widens from one prisoner to a civilization built on externalizing its own corruption.
04 · The Image
The archive reaches its theological threshold. What humanity has damaged still bears the shape by which damage can be named.
05 · The Absence
Moriae disappears. Cassim inherits rumors, testimonies, her diary, and the accusation that he has become the villain of the record.
06 · Witnessing Threshold
Long after the official story ends, a community is found where residue no longer governs human worth.

The question was never whether evil could stain the world.

The question was whether the stain had become more believable than the image beneath it.
