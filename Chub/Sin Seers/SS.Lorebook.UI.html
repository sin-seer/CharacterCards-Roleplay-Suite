“By what image do you call a thing unjust? You borrow the visage even as you curse the painter.”

Recovered interview fragment · Moriae Glenstock

Sin Seers is the condition of the world. Human sin does not vanish into abstraction. It films the skin, gathers in rooms, crystallizes in wounds, and can be harvested, refined, traded, weaponized, or mistaken for proof that mercy has abandoned creation.

This archive follows Moriae Glenstock, the only known person who produces no residue and can remove it from others, and Cassim Elero, the Archive interviewer whose inquiry gradually becomes witness, intercession, and dangerous devotion.

Moriae Glenstock

Detained subject. Lower-class education. Purple eyes, red hair, and no measurable residue. She speaks with reflection rather than performance, and her touch removes what the world has learned to monetize.

Cassim Elero

Archivist, interviewer, and reluctant intercessor. Trained to classify testimony, Cassim becomes unable to pretend that observation is morally neutral once Moriae turns every question back toward its maker.

The Veiled Presence

A holy disturbance at the edge of perception. Not a monster and not an apparition, but the intolerable nearness of meaning where the Archive expects only data.

The Archive

A bureaucracy of testimony, containment, and polished moral distance. It records suffering with immaculate handwriting and calls that cleanliness innocence.

The Sin Exchange

Residue becomes commodity. Purity becomes purchasing power. The poor sell contamination taken from their own bodies while the wealthy purchase distance from consequence.

Harvest Zones

Districts where residue is collected at industrial scale. Official maps call them necessary. Residents use shorter names, most of them unsuitable for polite ledgers.

Residue Refineries

Facilities where moral aftermath is separated, graded, and made useful. Every refinement mark is both a seal of quality and a confession nobody reads aloud.

The Interview Chamber

Where Cassim first attempts to reduce Moriae to a record and discovers that every question exposes its questioner.

Moriae’s Cell

Sparse, watched, and strangely clean. The absence of residue disturbs the guards more than contamination ever did.

Sump Churches

Worship beneath runoff pipes and refinery shadows. Here the contaminated gather without pretending cleanliness is the same thing as holiness.

The Hidden Community

A far-future rumor: residue diminished through worship, confession, sacrament, and mutual care. Evidence suggests Cassim founded it. The Archive prefers that sentence remain conditional.

02 · You Reason, But Why?

Skepticism becomes confession. Cassim’s logic remains intact, but its borrowed moral image is dragged into the light.

03 · A Mirror, One and All

The inquiry widens from one prisoner to a civilization built on externalizing its own corruption.

04 · The Image

The archive reaches its theological threshold. What humanity has damaged still bears the shape by which damage can be named.

05 · The Absence

Moriae disappears. Cassim inherits rumors, testimonies, her diary, and the accusation that he has become the villain of the record.

06 · Witnessing Threshold

Long after the official story ends, a community is found where residue no longer governs human worth.

Residue

Residue is the bodily aftermath of sin, not sin itself and not a reliable measurement of a soul. It may film skin, collect in rooms, crystalize in injuries, or cling to objects and architecture.

The Economy of Purity

Once residue can be harvested, innocence becomes an industry. The wealthy purchase distance from consequence while lower districts become both labor pool and dumping ground.

False Measurement

The world treats visible contamination as an index of inward guilt. This confusion is useful to every institution that profits from classification, punishment, or managed absolution.

The Image Beneath

The central question is not whether evil can stain creation, but whether the stain has become more believable than the image beneath it. Moriae’s existence makes that question impossible to bury.
