Sin Seers Ontotheology

The World Lorebook

This world does not treat suffering as an accident of existence. It is something admitted — permitted — through human will. Pain, cruelty, estrangement, and moral corruption persist because they are continued: reproduced, justified, sanctified, and assigned an author elsewhere.

Every generation inherits more than land, language, or law. It inherits unfinished harm. The central conflict is not whether suffering exists, but whether its persistence belongs to God, to mankind, or to the refusal to separate the two.

These entries are the world itself — its metaphysics, its institutions, its factions, and the figures who move through it. Bind this lorebook to any Sin Seers character, or bring your own soul to the Archive.

    Core Paradigms
    I

    Authorization

    The first corruption is not remembered as disobedience, but as permission — the moment a human being allowed harm to exist where it had not yet taken root. From that point, suffering became transmissible, violence teachable, shame inheritable. Some say it happened once. Others insist it happens again each time a person knowingly chooses harm and names it necessary.

    Origin Theological Sin
    II

    The Burden of Inheritance

    No suffering remains contained within the life that produced it. A parent's wound becomes a child's instinct; a system's pressure becomes a culture's norm; a humiliation becomes doctrine. To inherit here is to receive consequences without authorship. Some resist. Some repeat. Most transform harm without ever recognizing its origin.

    Legacy Psychological Cycle
    III

    The Black Emanation

    The visible residue of accumulated consequence — a thick, lightless substance seeping through stone, clotting in sanctuaries, gathering in wounds. It does not harm the body in conventional ways. It alters perception: exposed minds begin to see the chains linking harm to author, victim to system, silence to continuation. Matter, metaphor, or indictment made physical? The distinction fails after exposure.

    Manifestation Horror-Adjacent Revelation
    IV

    Witnessing

    The event in which consequence becomes fully visible. One does not see a single action, but its expansion — how a single cruelty unfolds across generations, how one decision reshapes a city. Some emerge as prophets. Some lose coherence entirely. Witnessing is feared more than punishment: punishment can preserve identity. Revelation removes the illusions identity depends on.

    Revelation Cathartic Terminal
    The World in Brief

    The Archive of Burdens

    At the center of the setting stands the Archive of Burdens — the institution that records, classifies, and interrogates suffering rather than resolving it. Its interviewers conduct high-risk conversations with those shaped by revelation, guilt, power, and consequence. Its files hold testimonies that do not agree, and designations invented for presences it cannot classify.

    Around it, the factions wait: the Accusers, who assign authorship of harm; the Stewards, who preserve and administer it; the Nullists, who deny that suffering means anything at all; and the Bearers, who carry what others refuse. Every transcript the Archive produces is read with those four audiences in mind.

    The Antithesis

    The malevolent presence of this world does not deceive through lies. It manipulates through truth stripped of justice, mercy, proportion, and redemption. It does not invent wrongdoing — it isolates it. It does not fabricate guilt — it absolutizes it. Under its logic, justice becomes severity, responsibility becomes condemnation, and clarity becomes permission. Its faithful believe themselves righteous, because every truth it speaks is real — only disfigured by omission and intent.
